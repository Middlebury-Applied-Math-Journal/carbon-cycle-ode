For most of my life, I was indi!erent towards math – towards all of my classes, really. In each, I saw my friends (fun), and in each, I had to do homework (blah). End of story. 

Then, in middle school, we spent a month going over and over what an equals sign meant. I was outraged! I think anyone would be. I was so bored, and so angry. Simultaneously, I was taking a separate math class where we were learning probability. I don’t like probability either. It feels made up. All this to say, I went from ‘indi!erent’ to ‘passionately against’ pretty quickly. But math rebounded for me in high school. I had a great, weird teacher in my freshman year. He asked us questions about building statues out of butter, and surrounding them with ponds full of frogs. The thing is, I don’t even remember the math we used to answer that. That was never really the point – he taught us how to think. 

Math is creativity. It’s not algebra, geometry, or calculous; these are parts of math. Methods, maybe. But math is curiosity. It’s looking at the world, and thinking, can I predict what happens next? Can I describe what I’m seeing through numbers? It’s problem solving, deductive reasoning. It’s the unraveling of a mystery. The people who know I’m a math major, but aren’t mathematicians themselves, are quick to make jokes when I count on my fingers. When I tell someone I’ve never memorized the times tables, I get, ‘aren’t you getting a degree in math?’

‘Yes’, I usually reply. ‘In math, not arithmetic.’ It’s true that I’m pretty comfortable with a fraction, and will do an integral for fun, but I’m just as likely to complain about a messy bit of algebra or trigonometry. Still, I haven’t found anything more satisfying than finishing a math problem and knowing it just looks right. When someone tells me they don’t like math, I usually ask, ‘well, do you like Sudoku?’ We’ve been taught that math is hard, inaccessible, and boring. But in reality, math is like Sudoku. It’s a mental exercise rooted in deductive organization. And I don’t think I’ve ever met someone who doesn’t enjoy a Sudoku puzzle every once in a while.
